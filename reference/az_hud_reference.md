## PART 1 — Arizona Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (A.R.S. Title 33, Chapter 10)

### 1.1 Statutory structure (🟢 PRIMARY — azleg.gov)

Source: https://www.azleg.gov/arsDetail/?title=33

- Article 1 — General Provisions: §§ 33-1301 to 33-1319
- Article 2 — Landlord Obligations: §§ 33-1321 to 33-1332
- Article 3 — Tenant Obligations: §§ 33-1341 to 33-1344
- Article 4 — Remedies: §§ 33-1361 to 33-1379
- Article 5 — Retaliatory Action: § 33-1381

### 1.2 Security Deposits — A.R.S. § 33-1321 (🟢 PRIMARY, verbatim)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01321.htm

> A. A landlord shall not demand or receive security, however denominated, including prepaid rent in an amount or value of more than one and one-half month's rent. This subsection does not prohibit a tenant from voluntarily paying more than one and one-half month's rent in advance.
>
> B. The purpose of all nonrefundable fees or deposits shall be stated in writing by the landlord. Any fee or deposit not designated as nonrefundable is refundable.
>
> C. On move in, a landlord shall furnish the tenant with a signed copy of the lease, a move-in form for specifying any existing damages to the dwelling unit and written notification to the tenant that the tenant may be present at the move-out inspection. On request by the tenant, the landlord shall notify the tenant when the landlord's move-out inspection will occur. If the tenant is being evicted for a material and irreparable breach and the landlord has reasonable cause to fear violence or intimidation on the part of the tenant, the landlord has no obligation to conduct a joint move-out inspection with the tenant.
>
> D. On termination of the tenancy, property or money held by the landlord as prepaid rent and security may be applied to the payment of all rent, and subject to a landlord's duty to mitigate, all charges as specified in the signed lease agreement, or as provided in this chapter, including the amount of damages which the landlord has suffered by reason of the tenant's noncompliance with section 33-1341. Within fourteen days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays or other legal holidays, after termination of the tenancy and delivery of possession and demand by the tenant, the landlord shall provide the tenant an itemized list of all deductions together with the amount due and payable to the tenant, if any. Unless other arrangements are made in writing by the tenant, the landlord shall mail the itemized list and any amount due, by first class mail, to the tenant's last known place of residence. If the tenant does not dispute the deductions or the amount due within sixty days after the itemized list is mailed, the amount is deemed valid and final and any further claims of the tenant are waived.
>
> E. If the landlord fails to comply with subsection D, the tenant may recover the property and money due together with damages equal to twice the amount wrongfully withheld.
>
> F. This section does not preclude the landlord or tenant from recovering other damages to which they may be entitled under this chapter.
>
> G. During the term of tenancy the landlord may use refundable deposits in accordance with any applicable property management agreement. At the end of tenancy, all refundable deposits shall be refunded pursuant to this section.
>
> H. The holder of the landlord's interest at the time of termination is bound by this section.

**Key takeaways:**
- Deposit cap: 1.5× monthly rent (voluntary overpayment allowed).
- Itemized deduction list deadline: 14 days, excluding Saturdays/Sundays/legal holidays.
- Deductions for damage are tied directly to tenant noncompliance with § 33-1341.
- Tenant has 60 days from mailing of the itemized list to dispute; after that, deductions are "deemed valid and final."
- Penalty for landlord noncompliance: 2× the wrongfully withheld amount.

### 1.3 Definitions — A.R.S. § 33-1310 (🟢 PRIMARY, verbatim excerpt)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01310.htm

> 15. "Security" means money or property given to assure payment or performance under a rental agreement. Security does not include a reasonable charge for redecorating or cleaning.

**"Normal wear and tear" is NOT a defined term anywhere in Chapter 10** (🔴 GAP — see 1.8). The exclusion of "a reasonable charge for redecorating or cleaning" from the definition of "security" implies such a charge is conceptually separate from the capped, refundable deposit — though it must still be reasonable and, if nonrefundable, disclosed in writing per § 33-1321(B).

### 1.4 Tenant's Duty to Maintain the Dwelling Unit — A.R.S. § 33-1341 (🟢 PRIMARY, full text)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01341.htm

> The tenant shall:
> 1. Comply with all obligations primarily imposed upon tenants by applicable provisions of building codes materially affecting health and safety.
> 2. Keep that part of the premises that he occupies and uses as clean and safe as the condition of the premises permit.
> 3. Dispose from his dwelling unit all ashes, rubbish, garbage and other waste in a clean and safe manner.
> 4. Keep all plumbing fixtures in the dwelling unit or used by the tenant as clean as their condition permits.
> 5. Use in a reasonable manner all electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning and other facilities and appliances including elevators in the premises.
> 6. Not deliberately or negligently destroy, deface, damage, impair or remove any part of the premises or knowingly permit any person to do so.
> 7. Conduct himself and require other persons on the premises with his consent to conduct themselves in a manner that will not disturb his neighbors' peaceful enjoyment of the premises.
> 8. Promptly notify the landlord in writing of any situation or occurrence that requires the landlord to provide maintenance or make repairs or otherwise requires the landlord to take action as prescribed in section 33-1324.

**This is the closest thing Arizona statute has to a legal standard for chargeable damage** — item 6 ("deliberately or negligently destroy, deface, damage, impair or remove"). By negative implication, ordinary deterioration that is not deliberate or negligent is not a proper basis for a deposit deduction.

### 1.5 Landlord's Duty to Maintain Fit Premises — A.R.S. § 33-1324 (🟢 PRIMARY, key excerpt)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01324.htm

> A. The landlord shall: ... 4. Maintain in good and safe working order and condition all electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning and other facilities and appliances, including elevators, supplied or required to be supplied by him.

Landlord and tenant may agree in writing, for adequate consideration (subsections C/D), that the tenant will perform certain of the landlord's maintenance duties (e.g., HVAC filter changes) — but only in good faith, not to evade core habitability obligations.

**Relevance:** Ordinary appliance/HVAC failure due to age is presumptively the landlord's responsibility unless the lease has validly shifted a specific task to the tenant.

### 1.6 Landlord's Remedies — A.R.S. § 33-1368 (🟢 PRIMARY, excerpt)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01368.htm

> C. The landlord may recover all reasonable damages resulting from noncompliance by the tenant with the rental agreement or section 33-1341 or occupancy of the dwelling unit, court costs, reasonable attorney fees and all quantifiable damage caused by the tenant to the premises.

### 1.7 Abandoned Property — A.R.S. § 33-1370 (🟢 PRIMARY, excerpt)

Source: https://azleg.gov/ars/33/01370.htm

Landlord holds tenant's abandoned property 14 calendar days after retaking possession; may charge actual removal/storage costs if tenant reclaims within 5 days of timely notice.

### 1.8 🔴 GAP: No statutory or case-law definition of "normal wear and tear" in Arizona

- A.R.S. § 33-1310 does not define "wear and tear" anywhere in Chapter 10.
- No citable published Arizona appellate case construing the term under § 33-1321/33-1341 was located.
- Industry blog claims about specific examples are 🟡 secondary paraphrases, not verified holdings.
- Arizona Dept. of Housing's compiled statute PDF (housing.az.gov) is a reproduction of the statute itself; site was bot-protected during research and could not be independently fetched — treat any added commentary there as unverified until manually confirmed.

### 1.9 Move-in/move-out inspection requirements

- Arizona's only requirement (§ 33-1321(C)): a written move-in form noting existing damage, plus written notice of tenant's right to be present at the move-out inspection.
- No statutory joint walkthrough requirement at move-in. Move-out walkthrough only if tenant requests it (narrow exception for irreparable-breach evictions with safety concerns).
- No mandated checklist content/format beyond "specifying any existing damages."

## PART 2 — HUD Guidance

**Threshold caveat:** HUD Handbook 4350.3, the Special Claims Processing Guide, and the Public Housing Occupancy Guidebook govern HUD-subsidized housing — **not binding law for private-market Arizona landlords**. Included because they are the most authoritative, quantified, industry-standard methodology for wear-and-tear vs. damage classification and useful-life proration. **Any HUD-derived tool output must be labeled "industry guidance, not Arizona statutory authority."**

### 2.1 HUD Handbook 4350.3 REV-1

Source: https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/43503hsgh.pdf

Model lease clause: "...deliver up and surrender said premises to the LANDLORD in as good condition as when received, reasonable wear and tear excepted."

**Note:** Change 4 (2013) removed Chapter 9's appendices from the Handbook itself. The useful-life table and damage examples now live only in the separate Special Claims Processing Guide (below) — cite that Guide directly, not "Handbook 4350.3 Appendix 5C/5D."

### 2.2 HUD Special Claims Processing Guide (HSG-06-01), June 2006 — Chapter 5

Source: https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/hsg-06-01gc5guid.pdf

Key provisions (🟢 PRIMARY, verbatim):
- § 5-2.E.2: "Only extraordinary repairs and/or replacements should be claimed. See Appendix 5C, 'Tenant Damages versus Normal Wear and Tear.'"
- § 5-2.E.5: "Failure to provide the tenant with an itemized listing of damages will result in the denial of the claim."
- § 5-3.D.2: "The owner/agent must certify the submitted claim is not the result of normal wear and tear or routine maintenance."
- § 5-3.D.3: Copies of move-in and move-out inspection reports are required documentation.
- § 5-3.D.5 (worked example): New carpet installed 2000 (5-yr life expectancy). Tenant occupied 2001–2003, carpet replaced 2 years early. **"The family should not be charged more than 2/5 of the cost of the new carpeting."** If carpet had been in service 5+ years at move-out, $0 chargeable.

**Proration formula:**
```
Max chargeable amount = Replacement/Repair Cost × (Remaining Useful Life Years ÷ Total Useful Life Years)
```
If an item is past its full useful life at move-out, the ordinary-deterioration charge is $0.

**Appendix 5C — Tenant Damage vs. Normal Wear and Tear (🟢 PRIMARY, verbatim, not all-inclusive):**

Normal Wear and Tear (landlord absorbs):
- Fading, peeling, or cracked paint
- Slightly torn or faded wallpaper
- Small chips in plaster
- Nail holes, pin holes, or cracks in wall
- Door sticking from humidity
- Cracked window pane from faulty foundation or building settling
- Floors needing a coat of varnish
- Carpet faded or worn thin from walking
- Loose grouting and bathroom tiles
- Worn or scratched enamel in old bathtubs, sinks, or toilets
- Rusty shower rod
- Partially clogged sinks caused by aging pipes
- Dirty or faded lamp or window shades

Tenant Damage (chargeable):
- Gaping holes in walls or plaster
- Drawings, crayon markings, or unapproved wallpaper
- Seriously damaged or ruined wallpaper
- Chipped or gouged wood floors
- Doors ripped off hinges
- Broken windows
- Missing fixtures
- Holes in ceiling from removed fixtures
- Holes, stains, or burns in carpet
- Missing or cracked bathroom tiles
- Chipped and broken enamel in bathtubs and sinks
- Clogged or damaged toilet from improper use
- Missing or bent shower rods
- Torn, stained, or missing lamp and window shades

Allowable "other charges" (§ 5-2.D.4.a): failure to return keys; late fees (if allowed by lease/local law); unpaid charges for damages already billed during tenancy.
**Not** allowable (§ 5-2.D.4.b): legal fees; collection agency fees; unpaid utility bills; cost of photographing the unit.

### 2.3 HUD Public Housing Occupancy Guidebook (2003)

Source: https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/doc_10760.pdf

Citing 24 CFR § 966.4(f): tenant must "pay reasonable charges (other than for normal wear and tear) for the repair of damages ... caused by the tenant, household members or guests."
Citing 24 CFR § 966.4(i): PHA must inspect at move-in and move-out with a written, signed condition statement; difference in condition (beyond normal wear and tear) establishes the basis for charges. Applies to Public Housing Agencies under 24 CFR Part 966 — narrower/different framework than Handbook 4350.3. No useful-life table found in this Guidebook.

## PART 4 — Sourcing Gaps and Flags (read before trusting tool output)

1. 🔴 No Arizona statutory definition of "normal wear and tear" exists. Operative statutory language is § 33-1341(6) ("deliberately or negligently destroy, deface, damage, impair or remove").
2. 🔴 No Arizona case law located construing "normal wear and tear" under this Act. Treat industry blog examples as informal consensus, not precedent.
3. 🟡 Arizona Dept. of Housing's compiled statute PDF could not be fetched directly (bot-protected) — should match azleg.gov text but any added commentary is unverified.
4. HUD sources are federal program-specific guidance for subsidized/public housing — not Arizona law, not binding on private-market AZ landlords. Label any HUD-derived figure in tool output as "industry guidance (HUD), not Arizona legal requirement."
5. Useful-life table and damage examples (Appendix 5C/5D) live in the Special Claims Processing Guide (HSG-06-01, 2006), not in current Handbook 4350.3 text (appendices removed 2013). Cite the Guide directly.
6. No HUD useful-life figure located for doors or cabinets — flagged as gaps rather than invented.
7. AZ statute requires only a written move-in damage form, not a joint walkthrough — narrower than many property managers assume.
